localNote: >-
  Coral Gables takes its tree canopy seriously. The city regulates the removal and relocation of trees on private property, so planting here is usually about adding to an established landscape rather than starting from bare ground. Live oaks, mahoganies, royal poincianas and old ficus throw deep shade over many lots, which decides what will actually thrive underneath: understory trees and shade-tolerant shrubs do well, while sun-loving palms and flowering trees belong at the edges or in open front yards. The ground is thin soil over limestone, so planting holes are dug wide rather than deep, the root flare is kept at grade, and new trees are staked so a summer storm does not rock them loose before the roots take hold.

Most Coral Gables installs are additions: a new shade tree where an old one came down, a row of privacy shrubs along a side yard, palms to frame an entry, or planting beds that finally match the house. The property already has a character, and the job is to add to it without a jarring gap in the canopy.

Every tree goes in with the root flare visible at the soil line, a wide planting hole cut into the limestone, mulch kept back from the trunk, and staking where the tree is tall enough to catch wind. Young trees that are planted and staked well before hurricane season stand a far better chance of coming through it upright.
