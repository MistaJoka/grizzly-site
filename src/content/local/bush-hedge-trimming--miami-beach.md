  Miami Beach is a barrier island, and the hedges show it. Salt spray and steady wind burn the exposed side of a hedge, so the plants that hold up here are the tough ones: clusia, sea grape, cocoplum, podocarpus and silver buttonwood. Lots are compact, with hedges doing the work of walls between neighbors and along the alley, and access is often a narrow side path, which changes how a crew sets up and how the clippings leave the property. Growth is fast from May through October, when a privacy hedge can put on inches in a few weeks, and slows in the cooler, drier months, so the trimming schedule should follow the season rather than the calendar.

On the beach, a hedge is doing a job: privacy from the neighbor a few feet away, a screen along the alley, a windbreak for the pool deck. Trimming keeps it dense from the ground up, which means cutting it slightly narrower at the top than the base so light reaches the lower growth, and taking off the salt-burned tips on the exposed side so new growth fills in.

Formal clusia and podocarpus hedges get a clean, flat face. Sea grape and cocoplum are shaped more loosely so they keep their natural look. Either way the job ends with the clippings gone and the walkways clean.
