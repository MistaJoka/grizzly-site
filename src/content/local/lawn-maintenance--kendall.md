  Kendall is classic South Florida suburbia: wide lots, big front lawns, long driveways and sidewalks, and a lot of St. Augustine grass. That turf is happiest cut high, around three and a half to four inches, and mowed often enough in summer that no more than a third of the blade comes off at once; cutting it short to stretch the interval is what turns a Kendall lawn brown and thin. Summer also brings chinch bugs and fungus to stressed St. Augustine, so a lawn that is mowed on schedule and edged cleanly is also a lawn that someone is looking at regularly and can flag a problem on early. A Kendall lawn is a lot of square footage, and the difference between a lawn that looks cared for and one that just looks cut is in the edges: crisp lines along the driveway, the sidewalk and the beds, trimmed around the mailbox and the fence posts, and the clippings blown off every hard surface.

A regular visit is also a regular look at the lawn. Brown patches, dry corners where a sprinkler head is missing, and the first signs of chinch bug damage are easier to catch early than after they spread across a lawn this size.
