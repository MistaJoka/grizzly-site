  Kendall is classic South Florida suburbia: wide lots, big front lawns, long driveways and sidewalks, and a lot of St. Augustine grass. That turf is happiest cut high, around three and a half to four inches, and mowed often enough in summer that no more than a third of the blade comes off at once; cutting it short to stretch the interval is what turns a Kendall lawn brown and thin. Summer also brings chinch bugs and fungus to stressed St. Augustine, so a lawn that is mowed on schedule and edged cleanly is also a lawn that is being looked at every week by someone who will notice a problem early. Townhome and condominium associations across the area add common lawns and long sidewalk strips to the same routine.

A Kendall lawn is a lot of square footage, and the difference between a lawn that looks cared for and one that just looks cut is in the edges: crisp lines along the driveway, the sidewalk and the beds, trimmed around the mailbox and the fence posts, and the clippings blown off every hard surface.

The weekly visit is also an inspection. Brown patches, dry corners where a sprinkler head is missing, and the first signs of chinch bug damage get spotted and mentioned before they spread across a lawn this size.
