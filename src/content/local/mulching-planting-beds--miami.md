localNote: >-
  Miami's planting beds sit on a few inches of soil over limestone, which drains fast, holds little water and tends alkaline. Mulch earns its keep here: a two to three inch layer keeps moisture in the root zone through the dry season, slows the weeds that explode after summer rain, and gives beds a finished edge that defines the whole front of a property. It needs to be kept a few inches back from trunks and stems, because mulch piled against bark holds water where it rots. Heavy rain washes loose mulch off sloped beds and onto walkways, so beds are edged and shaped to hold it, and the hurricane season from June through November is a reason to keep beds tidy rather than stacked with loose debris that becomes airborne in a storm.
faqs:
  - question: How deep should mulch be in Miami?
    answer: Two to three inches after settling, kept a few inches away from tree trunks and shrub stems. More than that smothers roots and holds too much water.
  - question: How often do beds need fresh mulch?
    answer: Once a year for most properties, usually before the dry season. Sun and rain break it down and wash it out faster here than in cooler climates.
  - question: Do you edge and weed the beds too?
    answer: Yes. Beds are weeded, re-cut at the edge and shaped to hold the mulch before the new layer goes down.

A fresh bed changes how a Miami property reads from the street: a clean edge against the lawn, dark mulch, and the plants standing out against it. The work is weeding and cutting the bed edge first, then shaping the surface so mulch stays put through the next downpour, then laying the mulch to depth and pulling it back from every trunk.

On commercial properties the same visit can cover the entrance beds and the strips along the parking areas, which are the first and last thing a visitor sees.
